# 🚀 **Tech & Policy: India’s Bold Moves & the Global AI Race**  

India is hitting **two strategic fronts** this week. Gujarat’s CM unveiled a **2047 vision** that places women, technology and inclusive growth at the core of the state’s long‑term plan — a rare political commitment that could accelerate gender‑balanced tech entrepreneurship if backed by real incentives【1】.  

At the same time, Nework’s **Google‑certified NewBoard E‑Series** promises to bring **connected learning** to classrooms, echoing Gujarat’s inclusive agenda and showing how private‑sector hardware can complement public policy【5】.  

On the national stage, India’s decision to **open its nuclear sector to proven foreign technology** under tight regulator oversight signals a pragmatic shift toward **global tech collaboration**, a move that could unlock faster clean‑energy deployment and inspire confidence among foreign investors【3】.  

Meanwhile, the **global AI battlefield** heats up: SpaceX’s $60 billion acquisition of Cursor marks **Elon Musk’s biggest AI bet**, directly challenging OpenAI and Anthropic. This underscores a broader trend—**mega‑capital flows into AI** that could reshape competitive dynamics for years to come【4】.  

Finally, **PCS Edventures** reporting a strong Q1 FY 2027 performance reflects the **robust appetite for ed‑tech** in Southeast Asia, reinforcing the idea that **learning‑tech ecosystems** are becoming a cornerstone of regional growth【2】.  

**Bottom line:** India’s dual focus on inclusive tech policy and strategic tech imports positions it as a **potential hub for sustainable innovation**, while the AI arms race abroad reminds us that staying ahead will require both **policy foresight** and **private‑sector agility**.
