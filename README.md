What happens in tech today (2026-10-29):

# Apple goes touchscreen, the FCC goes silent — devices are becoming policy

**Apple showed its first touchscreen laptops in a product blitz**, years after insisting nobody wanted touch on a MacBook. The delay is the story: **the company adopts a feature only after the market proves it** — iPads made touch normal, so laptops finally got it.

Meanwhile the **FCC is voting on an unprecedented order to jam cellphone signals in jails and prisons**. Jamming is not a tech problem, it is a governance one: **who decides that a signal is dangerous?** Prison cell blocking has failed for two decades because contraband phones smuggle in anyway. Regulation without enforcement is theatre.

Also today: Kingston's police chief welcomes board support for **body-worn cameras**, and the US Social Security chief floated a "first solution" to its funding crisis. Both show that **the tech news that matters is rarely on the gadget beat** — it is in the mundane decisions about cameras, signals and money.
